Spend vs. Outcomes

DOES SPENDING
ACTUALLY WIN GAMES?

Every NIL dollar is a bet that money buys wins. We ran the actual numbers — team NIL estimate against win percentage — for every school in the dataset with an NIL figure, sport by sport. The relationship is real, but it's weaker than the arms race narrative suggests.

NIL Spend vs. Win %
Football

Football shows the strongest relationship of the four sports, though it is still moderate. Texas, Miami, Ohio State, and Oregon all sit near the top of both axes. But the exceptions matter: LSU spent $42.8M and won just 53.8% of its games, Arkansas' $22M budget produced a 16.7% win rate — among the worst in the sample — and national champion Indiana went 16-0 on $21.1M, right around the median. Money buys talent, not scheme fit or health, and both of those still decide close games.

NIL Spend vs. Win %
Men's Basketball

Basketball shows the weakest relationship of the four sports. Kentucky's $22M collective is nearly double the next-highest budget, yet Duke and Michigan won at 92%+ clips on budgets of $10.5–12M. Basketball rosters are smaller and rotations tighter, so a well-coached team can out-execute a bigger spender far more easily than in football. Depth and matchups still carry more weight than payroll size in March.

NIL Spend vs. Win %
Men's Baseball

Georgia posted a 79.1% win rate on a $900K collective, outperforming several programs spending over $1M. Baseball's NIL market is still emerging and draft eligibility complicates who collectives can even commit to long-term, which likely keeps development and coaching more decisive here than in the two revenue sports.

NIL Spend vs. Win %
Women's Volleyball

Nebraska's $1.8M collective and 97.1% win rate anchor the top of both axes, but the pattern loosens further down the list: Penn State spent $750K and went 19-13, while SMU won 81.8% of its matches on $140K. Smaller rosters and lower per-athlete costs likely mean volleyball NIL dollars go further toward locking in a program's best players.

HOW THE FOUR SPORTS STACK UP

Pearson correlation coefficient (r) between team NIL spend and win percentage, by sport. Higher means NIL spend more reliably predicts winning; lower means other factors — coaching, depth, development — are doing more of the work.

Takeaway: Across all four sports, NIL spend explains of the variance in win rate. That's a meaningful edge — but nowhere close to deterministic.
