About the Project

WHERE DOES THE
MONEY GO?

Since the NCAA opened the door to NIL compensation in 2021, billions of dollars have moved through college athletics. Most of it with very little transparency.

NIL Index is a research project that centralizes and visualizes compensation data across sports, conferences, and schools. The core question: does spending more actually lead to winning more? The data will tell us.

This site sits at the intersection of sports business and data journalism — built as an honors thesis at the University of Arizona, with an AI-powered tool layered in to make the data accessible to anyone who wants to dig.

Data Methodology

HOW THE
NUMBERS WORK

NIL compensation is not centrally reported. The figures on this site are built from a combination of verified public data and structured estimates where official figures do not exist. Here is exactly how each number was produced.

Data Sources
On3.com
Primary source for individual player NIL valuations and team roster values. On3's proprietary algorithm combines reported deal data, collective contracts, and market comparables to estimate annual player compensation. Roster values aggregate individual valuations into a program total.
Sportico.com
Used for collective-level budget reporting and cross-referencing program spending. Sportico publishes annual NIL market analyses sourced from collective operators, athletic department disclosures, and industry insiders.
NCAA MFRS Reports
Schools are required to file annual financial reports (Membership Financial Reporting System) with the NCAA. These total operating expense figures — obtained via FOIA requests by outlets including Extra Points and Sportico — serve as anchor points for sports where direct NIL data is limited.
Collective & Athletic Department Disclosures
Where available, publicly reported collective budgets and school announcements about NIL fund commitments are incorporated directly as verified figures.
Estimation Methodology
Anchor Points
For each sport, known figures from verified sources establish baseline values for the highest-spending programs. These serve as the upper bounds from which all estimates are scaled downward.
Conference Tier Scaling
Programs are grouped by conference (Power 4, Group of 5, other) and assigned a multiplier range relative to anchor points. SEC and Big Ten programs are estimated at higher levels reflecting documented market premiums in recruiting and collective funding.
Program Prestige Modifiers
Within each conference tier, adjustments are made based on postseason performance, historical recruiting rankings, and publicly reported collective activity. National champions and CFP/NCAA Tournament programs receive upward adjustments.
Women's Volleyball Specific
Because direct NIL figures for volleyball are rarely disclosed, estimates use total operating expenses (from NCAA MFRS reports) as a proxy, allocating approximately 15–25% as the NIL component — consistent with reporting from schools where both figures are available.
Baseball-Specific Methodology
Baseball NIL is uniquely complex due to MLB draft eligibility — players can be drafted after their junior year (or out of high school), which shortens the window collectives have to retain talent and complicates year-over-year spending comparisons. Roster turnover driven by the draft, rather than NIL alone, is factored into how spending figures are interpreted relative to win totals.
Volleyball-Specific Methodology
Women's volleyball NIL data is the least publicly reported of the four tracked sports. Figures here lean most heavily on the operating-expense proxy method described above, cross-checked against any publicly announced collective commitments specific to volleyball programs.
!
Important — Understanding These Numbers: True NIL collective budgets are not publicly disclosed by nearly any school. Figures on this site combine verified reporting where it exists with structured estimates elsewhere, and are marked "ver." or "est." throughout the data tables accordingly. Treat every figure as directional, not exact — the goal is to show relative scale and trends across schools and conferences, not precise dollar amounts.
